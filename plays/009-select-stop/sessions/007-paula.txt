What the document now covers:
- **The shown stop (new section):** the whole screen follows one stop — the one the user tapped if there is one, otherwise the nearest. It explains how the tapped stop is held by id, why tapping the nearest stop shows it as "Nearest stop", and what happens when a refresh drops the tapped stop.
- **Selecting a stop (new section):**
  - how a tap picks a stop, within 16 px of the pointer;
  - taps on markers go through to the map, and panning never counts as a tap;
  - what tapping a stop or empty map does, and the pointer cursor on desktop;
  - two accepted limitations: double-tap zoom also counts as tapping away, and stops can't be selected with the keyboard.
- **Sheet:** a new first rule shows a selected stop even while the location is off or still being found. It has the `// SELECTED STOP` label, and the distance is left out when the location is unknown. The minimised sheet follows the same rule.
- **Map markers:** there is one stop marker, on the shown stop, labelled "Nearest stop" or "Selected stop". Bus countdowns and dimming follow the shown stop.
- **Smart zoom:** the default view frames the user, the nearest stop and its next buses. The selected view frames only the tapped stop and its next buses, without the user. The section also says:
  - the automatic first framing doesn't run while a stop is selected;
  - why framing is requested and run on the next render rather than called straight from the tap. Called straight away, it would frame the previous stop's buses.
- **Locate button:** clears any selection, and keeps it when retrying the location.
- **Direction filter:** resets whenever the shown stop changes.
- **Testing notes (new section):** how the end-to-end tests work out where a stop is on screen, and the rule to fix that calculation rather than widen the 16 px tap radius when a click misses.
